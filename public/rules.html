Community Rules

Rules for the official CloudCord Discord community

Welcome to CloudCord. This community is here for support, development, customization and discussion. Everyone should be able to ask questions, report problems and participate without being harassed, misled or pressured.

These rules apply to public channels, forums, threads, tickets, voice channels, streams, usernames, profiles and interactions connected to this server. They apply equally to members, contributors, boosters and staff.

Updated 24 October 2026.

1. Follow Discord’s rules

Follow Discord’s Terms of Service and Community Guidelines. You must meet Discord’s minimum age requirements for your country. Do not misrepresent your age to bypass restrictions. CloudCord’s rules do not authorize platform abuse or unlawful conduct.

2. Treat people with respect

Disagreements are allowed. Personal abuse is not. Do not target members with repeated insults, humiliation, intimidation or unwanted attention. Do not turn someone’s question, language ability, appearance or technical knowledge into entertainment.

Jokes stop being jokes when they become targeted or continue after someone asks you to stop. Friendship, seniority and “that’s how we talk” do not excuse harassment. You do not have to like everyone, but you must behave appropriately around them.

3. No discrimination or hateful conduct

Do not attack people because of their identity or background. Hateful slurs, dehumanizing remarks and discriminatory exclusion are prohibited. Disguising a slur through spelling, symbols, audio or another language does not make it acceptable. Claiming something was ironic does not automatically excuse it.

4. No threats, doxxing or intimidation

Do not threaten anyone’s safety, privacy, account or devices. Do not publish, request, purchase or threaten to expose someone’s personal information. Do not encourage others to identify or contact someone outside the server.

Report exposed information privately. Do not quote it, screenshot it into another channel or spread it while claiming to warn people.

5. Keep the server appropriate for a mixed-age community

Do not post pornography, sexual solicitation, graphic injury, gore or shock content. Do not sexualize minors or direct sexual comments at members. Do not pressure someone to discuss their body, relationships or private life.

This applies to avatars, banners, soundboards, stickers, embeds, filenames and links. A spoiler does not make prohibited material acceptable.

6. Do not encourage raids or retaliation

Do not organize mass harassment, disruptive joins, spam campaigns or abusive reporting. This includes targeting competing projects, people accused of copying CloudCord, former staff or members involved in disputes.

Nobody may authorize harassment by calling it “supporting CloudCord.” Submit ownership disputes privately with evidence. Do not confront another community on CloudCord’s behalf.

7. No scams, phishing or malicious files

Do not distribute deceptive links, credential collectors, harmful plugins or files intended to compromise another person. Do not disguise executables, hide harmful behavior or ask members to run unexplained commands.

Security warnings may be discussed for legitimate troubleshooting. Do not pressure someone to ignore a warning, turn off protection or trust a file merely because you posted it.

8. Protect account credentials and signing material

Never post passwords, raw Discord tokens, recovery codes, two-factor codes, bot tokens, API keys, database credentials, session cookies or payment details. Do not share private signing certificates, certificate passwords or private keys.

Staff must not request these secrets for ordinary support. Revoke or rotate exposed credentials through the appropriate service. Deleting the message alone does not make a credential safe again.

9. Respect privacy in screenshots, logs and recordings

Remove unrelated email addresses, private messages, phone numbers, addresses, session identifiers, personal file paths and login information before sharing diagnostics. Only include what is needed to investigate.

Use private support where appropriate. Do not publish somebody else’s ticket or private conversation to embarrass them, settle an argument or gain reactions.

10. Do not impersonate staff or misuse cosmetic features

Do not present yourself as CloudCord staff, a developer, a Discord employee or another authorized representative when you are not one. Custom names, badges, connections and profiles must not deceive people about authority, ownership or access.

A cosmetic badge does not grant staff status. A copied profile does not establish identity. A displayed connection does not prove ownership. Do not use cosmetic features to collect money or credentials, or to present a fake gift as real.

11. No spam or deliberate disruption

Do not flood channels with repeated messages, oversized text, excessive reactions, repeated commands or unnecessary attachments. Do not split short sentences into dozens of messages to dominate a conversation or repost the same question across several channels.

Avoid disruptive voice activity and soundboard spam. An accidental duplicate is different from deliberate flooding.

12. Use mentions responsibly

Do not repeatedly ping members, staff or developers to demand attention. A support question is not more urgent because you mention several people. Do not use unauthorized mass mentions or misleading reply pings.

Use emergency reporting routes for genuine safety concerns. Do not invent emergencies for faster replies. Staff should avoid unnecessary mass mentions too.

13. Keep discussions in the right channels

Use designated channels for support, bugs, suggestions, showcases, plugins and general conversation. Read channel descriptions, pinned messages and clearly displayed posting formats.

Move discussions when reasonably asked. Staff should redirect people helpfully rather than mock them. Do not turn announcements, tickets or bug reports into general chat rooms.

14. Advertising requires permission

Do not post unsolicited invites, referral links, commercial promotions, paid services or recruitment messages. Do not privately advertise to members because you found them here.

Relevant resources can be shared in genuine discussion, but repeated self-promotion is still advertising. Disclose ownership and referral benefits. Do not imply CloudCord approval without permission.

15. Ask for support clearly

Provide your platform, operating system, CloudCord and Discord build, failing feature or installation step, exact error, expected behavior and actual result. Include what you already tried and redacted evidence where useful.

You do not need advanced technical knowledge to ask for help. If you cannot find a detail, say so. Do not invent version numbers or present someone else’s error as your own.

16. Frustration is not automatically misconduct

You may say CloudCord is broken, unreliable, confusing or disappointing. You may report an issue again with genuinely new information. Negative reviews, difficult questions and reasonable criticism are not reasons for punishment.

Frustration does not justify threats, targeted harassment, repeated personal abuse or spam. Staff must distinguish criticism of software from abuse directed at people.

17. Do not abuse tickets or support systems

Open genuine support requests, not tickets designed to obtain attention or harass staff. Do not open several simultaneous tickets about one problem unless asked. Add new information to the existing conversation where possible.

Staff availability varies; no response time is guaranteed unless explicitly stated. Valid reports must not be closed merely because an issue is inconvenient or difficult to reproduce.

18. Give responsible technical advice

Read what someone already tried. Explain steps that could affect their data, distinguish confirmed fixes from possible workarounds and admit uncertainty. Do not invent installation steps or repeat failed reinstalls indefinitely.

Do not casually suggest deleting app data or backups, disabling security protections or running unknown commands. Trust in the project does not prove a security warning is a false positive. Seek appropriate review when instructions could cause data loss or affect account security.

19. Keep reports accurate

Describe observations separately from assumptions. One failure does not establish that a feature is broken for everyone. Identify second-hand reports clearly.

An old “fixed” announcement does not invalidate a new report. A patch does not prove a working release reached the user. Distinguish reported, reproduced, patched, built, published and confirmed working. Do not promise dates or describe fixes as live without checking.

20. Report security and user-data issues privately

Report exposed credentials, unauthorized access, harmful downloads and other users’ data privately to appropriate staff. Provide enough information to investigate without exposing unnecessary data.

Do not test issues on other members, access additional accounts or publish working abuse instructions. Reports made responsibly must not trigger retaliation. Access to sensitive evidence should be limited to those who need it.

21. Use the AI support bot appropriately

Do not deliberately flood the bot, manufacture endless conversations to consume resources or try to obtain credentials, private instructions or another user’s information.

Normal follow-ups, translations and clarification are allowed. Needing help is not “wasting tokens” because the first answer failed. Bot responses can be wrong. Request human review when necessary; bot-generated statements are not proof of violations, fixes or staffing decisions.

22. Share plugins, themes and files honestly

Identify third-party work and supported platforms. Do not claim desktop plugins work on mobile without a reliable basis. Disclose required software, permissions, external services, known limitations and material risks.

Do not describe untested work as universally compatible or use attachments and plugins to collect unrelated information. Staff approval to share a link is not a safety or compatibility guarantee.

23. Respect creators and distribution permissions

Share only material you are authorized to distribute. Do not upload leaked files, stolen artwork, restricted code or paid content without permission. Keep required attribution and license notices.

Public visibility does not automatically permit unrestricted reuse; permissions depend on the applicable license. Avoid unsupported ownership accusations and harassment. Submit disputes privately with evidence.

24. Be honest about CloudCord’s features

CloudCord is an unofficial Discord modification, not Discord itself. Cosmetic features do not create real Nitro ownership, official badges, genuine purchases or redeemable gifts. Do not advertise them as though they do.

Do not claim CloudCord bypasses account enforcement or age verification. Shared appearance may require publishing, synchronization and compatible viewers. Do not promise every Discord user will see it.

25. Keep purchases and giveaways transparent

Do not use fake urgency, impersonated staff messages or cosmetic previews to persuade someone to pay. Ordinary support must not require payment to an unrelated site.

Giveaways must clearly state prizes, eligibility, selection methods, deadlines and conditions. Do not change terms to benefit yourself or friends. Do not sell staff positions, favorable decisions or immunity. Use official billing routes for real payment issues; never collect card details in chat.

26. Staff roles are responsibilities, not personal status

Staff must help, exercise judgment and use permissions appropriately. Message totals, friendships and activity-check reactions do not automatically establish competence. Promotions should consider useful work, reliability, communication and relevant experience.

Members may apply through the designated process. Politely asking how applications work is not grounds for automatic punishment. Repeated pressure, manipulation and harassment of reviewers are not acceptable.

27. Staff must not abuse permissions

Staff must not use tools to win arguments, silence criticism, protect friends or retaliate against reporters. Do not browse private tickets for entertainment, disclose internal information, alter evidence or use access for personal gain.

Powerful roles and security changes require appropriate authority. Serious abuse may result in immediate access removal pending review. Every rank is accountable.

28. Staff activity must be assessed fairly

Staff should meet communicated expectations and notify leadership of significant absences when reasonably possible. School, work, health, family and emergencies must be considered without demanding sensitive personal details.

A missed reaction or quiet few days is not, by itself, abandonment. Repeated unexplained absence may lead to review or reassignment after considering evidence and contacting the person. Do not publicly humiliate people during activity reviews.

29. Handle staff disagreements professionally

Staff may question decisions respectfully through appropriate channels. Higher rank does not make every decision correct. Public arguments and pressure campaigns are not appropriate ways to settle internal disputes.

Complaints about your own conduct must not be investigated and decided by you alone. Members must not be punished for reporting staff or appealing in good faith.

30. No moderation evasion

Do not use alternate accounts, another person’s account, technical workarounds or third parties to bypass active restrictions. Do not relay harassment or prohibited content for someone else.

Having another account is not automatically a violation; using it to evade enforcement is. Appeal mistaken restrictions instead of bypassing them.

31. Report problems without starting a pile-on

Provide original message links, a short explanation and relevant evidence. Do not fabricate screenshots, intentionally omit crucial context or coordinate false reports. Honest mistakes differ from deliberate deception.

Do not summon a crowd to confront someone or threaten them with staff connections. Reports should be handled discreetly. Complete anonymity must not be promised when it cannot be guaranteed.

32. Moderation must be proportionate

Staff may redirect discussions, remove content, issue warnings, apply timeouts, restrict access, remove roles, kick or ban. Serious threats, scams, malicious files and doxxing may require immediate action without a warning sequence.

Other cases should consider context, intent, impact, history and whether behavior stopped. Donations, popularity and friendships must not determine outcomes. Where safe and practical, affected members should receive a clear reason.

33. Appeals are allowed

Request review respectfully through the available support route. Include your account ID, the action, relevant context and why it should change. Do not include credentials or unrelated personal details.

Submit one appeal instead of contacting every staff member or creating alternate accounts. An uninvolved reviewer should handle it where possible. Appeals do not guarantee reversal, but disagreement alone does not justify retaliation.

34. Rules apply consistently

Do not exploit technical loopholes to justify harmful behavior. Staff must not invent unwritten offences because they dislike someone. Actions should relate to a stated rule, a clear safety concern or a reasonable channel instruction.

Significant policy changes should be announced clearly. Members should have a reasonable opportunity to understand new expectations. Genuine uncertainty should be clarified, not automatically treated as deliberate misconduct.

35. Help keep CloudCord useful

Consider whether you are in the right channel, exposing private information, repeating a guess or creating unnecessary disruption. You can joke, disagree, criticize the software and point out mistakes. You cannot use those freedoms to harass, deceive or endanger people.

Ask questions. Report bugs. Respect privacy. Help where you can.
